Topic: Internal Registries of nn.Module
Why do they exist?

To organize the important parts of a model so PyTorch can efficiently inspect, move, save, load, and traverse it.

The Three Registries
Registry	Stores
_parameters	Trainable nn.Parameter objects
_modules	Child nn.Module objects
_buffers	Non-trainable tensors that are still part of the model's state

Everything else is just a normal Python attribute.

Mental Model

Think of nn.Module as maintaining three organized dictionaries rather than scanning every attribute repeatedly.

Why does this matter?

These registries power methods like:

parameters()
named_parameters()
modules()
named_modules()
state_dict()
to()
train()
eval()
Takeaway

nn.Module doesn't just store attributes—it classifies and organizes important objects into internal registries. Those registries are the foundation for almost all of PyTorch's model-management features.

Why this lesson matters

At first glance, methods like model.parameters() or model.state_dict() can seem like independent APIs.

Now you can see they're not.

They're simply different ways of using the same internal registries.

That realization is what starts turning PyTorch from a collection of functions into a coherent system.
