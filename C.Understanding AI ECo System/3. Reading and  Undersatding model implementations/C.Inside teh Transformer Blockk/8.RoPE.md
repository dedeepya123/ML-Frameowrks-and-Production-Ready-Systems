# Module 4 — Inside the Transformer Block

## Lesson 8 — Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE)

## 1. Why positional information is needed

Self-attention compares Q and K representations but does not inherently know the order of tokens.

Therefore Transformer architectures need a mechanism to represent token position.

Gemma uses Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE).

---

## 2. Core idea

RoPE does not add a positional embedding vector to the token embedding.

Instead:

Q → position-dependent rotation
K → position-dependent rotation
V → unchanged

The rotated Q/K are then used in the attention calculation.

---

## 3. Why Q and K are rotated

Attention is:

softmax(QKᵀ / sqrt(D)) V

Q and K determine which tokens attend to which other tokens.

V contains the information being retrieved.

Therefore RoPE is applied to Q and K, not V.

---

## 4. Basic rotation

For a 2D vector:

[x, y]

rotation by angle θ:

x' = x cos(θ) - y sin(θ)

y' = x sin(θ) + y cos(θ)

RoPE applies this idea to pairs of dimensions inside the head dimension.

Example:

Q = [q0,q1,q2,q3]

Treat as:

[q0,q1]
[q2,q3]

and rotate each pair.

---

## 5. Position determines rotation

The rotation angle depends on:

position × frequency

Conceptually:

theta(position, i) = position × omega_i

Different dimension pairs use different frequencies.

Therefore:

- position changes the rotation angle
- different dimension pairs rotate at different frequencies

This allows multiple positional scales to be represented.

---

## 6. Why cos and sin appear in implementations

The rotation equations directly require:

cos(theta)
sin(theta)

Therefore implementations commonly contain tensors/functions such as:

cos
sin
inv_freq
position_ids

These correspond directly to the mathematics of RoPE.

---

## 7. Shape behavior

RoPE does not change the tensor shape.

Before RoPE:

Q = [B, heads, S, D]
K = [B, heads, S, D]

After RoPE:

Q' = [B, heads, S, D]
K' = [B, heads, S, D]

Only the values change.

---

## 8. Why RoPE is useful

Suppose:

token A is at position 5
token B is at position 8

Their rotations differ according to:

8 - 5 = 3

The mathematical structure of RoPE allows Q/K interactions to represent relative positional relationships.

This is one reason RoPE works naturally with attention.

---

## 9. Where RoPE appears

Attention flow:

hidden_states
    ↓
q_proj / k_proj / v_proj
    ↓
Q / K / V
    ↓
reshape into heads
    ↓
RoPE(Q,K)
    ↓
Q' / K'
    ↓
Q'K'ᵀ
    ↓
scale
    ↓
causal mask
    ↓
softmax
    ↓
attention weights × V
    ↓
combine heads
    ↓
o_proj

---

## 10. Common implementation concepts

### rotary_emb

Produces the positional rotation information, commonly represented through cos/sin values.

### position_ids

Represent the sequence positions.

### inv_freq

Represents the frequencies used for different dimension pairs.

### cos / sin

Rotation coefficients.

### rotate_half

An implementation technique for expressing the pairwise rotation efficiently.

### apply_rotary_pos_emb

Applies the positional rotation to Q and K.

---

## 11. Important code-reading rule

When seeing:

cos, sin = rotary_emb(position_ids)

think:

"Generate position-dependent rotation coefficients."

When seeing:

query_states, key_states = apply_rotary_pos_emb(...)

think:

"Inject positional information into Q and K."

When seeing:

query_states @ key_states.transpose(...)

think:

"Now calculate attention using position-aware Q and K."

---

## 12. Mental model

RoPE is not:

token_embedding + position_embedding

Instead:

token representation
    ↓
Q/K projection
    ↓
position-dependent rotation
    ↓
position-aware Q/K
    ↓
attention

---

## 13. One-sentence summary

RoPE injects positional information into Q and K by rotating their dimension pairs according to token position, allowing Q-K interactions to capture positional/relative relationships.

## RopE Mental model
1. Project hidden states into Q/K.

2. Split Q/K into attention heads.

3. Each head has head_dim dimensions.

4. Pair the dimensions:
   (0,1), (2,3), (4,5), ...

5. Each pair has a fixed frequency.

6. For token position p:
   angle = p × frequency.

7. Calculate:
   cos(angle)
   sin(angle)

8. Use cos/sin to rotate each Q/K dimension pair.

9. V is not rotated.

10. The resulting Q'/K' are used in:
    Q'K'ᵀ

11. Therefore positional information affects
    which tokens attend to which tokens.


    ### Note

Different model implementations can store/reshape the cos and sin tensors differently—[S, D/2], expanded to [S, D], or arranged to match a particular rotate_half convention.

So when we open the actual GemmaRotaryEmbedding code, we should trace the exact shapes line by line rather than memorizing a generic RoPE implementation.
